## This is a maintenance decision, not a verdict on WordPress

WordPress remains capable, actively maintained software. It can support publishing teams, memberships, learning systems, ecommerce, multilingual sites, and specialized workflows through its large plugin ecosystem.

That flexibility comes with responsibility for hosting, core updates, themes, plugins, backups, security, and compatibility. A well-managed installation can be fast and dependable. The question is whether that ownership model still suits the organization.

## When Webflow may fit better

A move is worth considering when the site is primarily a marketing or content website, editors want a visual CMS, and plugin maintenance has become unnecessary overhead.

[Webflow](/platforms/webflow) manages the hosting infrastructure and platform updates. It does not require the client to maintain a WordPress server, theme, or plugin stack. That can simplify ownership, although Webflow still has plan costs, platform limits, and connected services that need attention.

## Assess the functionality before the content

Pages and posts are usually the straightforward part. The important questions are what the plugins, theme, and custom code currently do.

Before recommending a move, we review:

- Pages, posts, media, categories, tags, and custom post types
- Forms, search, redirects, analytics, and SEO settings
- Membership, login, booking, ecommerce, multilingual, and gated-content features
- Integrations with CRM, email, payments, calendars, and outside databases
- Current traffic, established URLs, backlinks, and Search Console data

WordPress content models do not always have direct Webflow equivalents. Taxonomies, relationships, shortcodes, embeds, and complex rich content may need restructuring or cleanup rather than a mechanical import.

## When staying is the right answer

Stay on WordPress when the site depends on functionality that Webflow cannot replace economically, when a publishing team already uses it effectively, or when qualified technical staff maintain it well.

A large WooCommerce store, learning platform, complex membership system, or specialized editorial workflow may be better improved in place. The useful recommendation may be better hosting, fewer plugins, an updated theme, or clearer maintenance responsibility.

## Rebuilding and protecting continuity

The design is recreated in Webflow rather than converted from the WordPress theme. Content is imported, cleaned, or rebuilt according to its structure. Forms and integrations are recreated and tested.

Established URLs remain unchanged where practical. Redirects are used for relevant changes and deliberate consolidation, not as a substitute for a content plan. Metadata, structured data, internal links, canonicals, sitemaps, and indexing controls are reviewed before launch.

Our redesign for [3D Security & Training Solutions](/works/3d-sts) included a move from WordPress to Webflow, along with an embedded class calendar managed through Square.

Most WordPress-to-Webflow projects take four to eight weeks. Site size, content cleanup, custom post types, and functionality determine where a project falls in that range. We provide a fixed scope after reviewing the live site and administrative setup.
