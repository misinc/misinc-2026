Luna Rossa was founded in 2001 by Paolo and Sylvia D'Andrea, with vineyards in the Mimbres Valley. Paolo grew up in Friuli in north-east Italy, in the fourth generation of a family of wine growers, which is a more direct line to New Mexico winemaking than most people expect.

The business is now a winery, two tasting rooms (the second in Mesilla), a wine club, a pizzeria, and a national online shop.

## Selling wine online is a compliance problem

The interesting part of this build is not the storefront. It is that shipping wine across state lines in the United States is governed separately by every state, and the rules are not similar to each other.

Some states accept direct shipment. Some prohibit it. Some allow it for certain products and not others. Getting this wrong is not a poor customer experience; it is a licensing problem for the winery.

So availability is handled explicitly rather than being left for checkout to discover. The shop states plainly which states it ships to, flags the products with narrower restrictions, and offers on-site pickup where that is the answer. A customer finds out before they have filled a basket, which is the only humane place to tell them.

## A winery is several businesses

Wine sales, tastings, the club, production, party planning, and a pizzeria are genuinely different operations with different customers. Somebody booking a table is not the person joining the wine club.

The navigation separates them rather than filing everything under a single "visit" heading, so each has room to explain itself.

## Restraint in the design

Wine sites tend toward heavy ornament. This one stays quiet: generous space, a serif for the storytelling, and photography of the bottles doing the selling.

The product listings carry what a wine buyer actually wants: varietal, appellation, vintage, alcohol content, and bottle size. For this customer those are not specifications, they are the description.
