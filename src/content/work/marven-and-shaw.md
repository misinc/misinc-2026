Marven & Shaw run complex investigations into corruption, money laundering, and fraud. They file civil complaints alongside leading law firms and support law enforcement agencies and civil society partners.

## Confidence without theatrics

Almost every visual convention available to this category is wrong for it. Investigations work has an established aesthetic of dark backgrounds, surveillance imagery, and a general air of the thriller, and all of it reads as performance.

The people who hire this firm are lawyers, agencies, and whistleblowers, and every one of them is assessing seriousness. A site that looks like a film poster suggests a firm that thinks about how the work looks rather than whether it stands up in court.

So the design is restrained and typographic, and the case is made in the writing. Their three stated pillars of experience, service, and values are given as plain claims a reader can weigh, not decorated.

## The history section earns its place

The site opens its history in 1777, with ten sailors in the new American navy who reported their commodore and became, in effect, the first whistleblowers in the country.

That is an unusual thing to put on a professional services site, and it does more work than a mission statement could. It says what the firm believes about who takes the risk in this kind of case, and it does it in a story rather than an adjective. We gave it real space for that reason.

## Three doors

The services split cleanly by who is arriving: whistleblower complaints, boutique investigations, and law enforcement support. A whistleblower and a federal agency are in very different positions and need different reassurance, so each route is separate and reachable from the homepage.
