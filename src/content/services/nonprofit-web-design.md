## Your website has to convince two different audiences

A donor deciding whether to give and a family deciding whether to ask you for help are reading the same page for completely different reasons. Most nonprofit sites are written for the board that approved them, and end up convincing neither.

We have built sites for organizations ranging from a single-scholarship nonprofit to a national election-policy group, and the pattern holds every time: the site's job is to turn belief in a mission into something concrete, a donation, a volunteer sign-up, a grant reviewer's confidence, without losing the people the organization actually exists to serve.

## What we build for nonprofits

- **Donation and membership tools** that do not take an unreasonable cut or bury the "give" button three clicks deep.
- **Impact reporting written for grant reviewers**, not just visitors: the numbers, outcomes, and credibility markers a funder is actually scanning for.
- **Volunteer and event sign-ups** that produce a usable list, not a spreadsheet someone has to clean up by hand.
- **A site your staff can update**, because nonprofit staff and board turnover is real, and a site only one departed volunteer knew how to edit is a liability.
- **Board- and funder-facing credibility** alongside a front door that still welcomes the community you serve, on the same site, without one undermining the other.

## Pricing that fits how nonprofits actually operate

We know the budget conversation is different for a 501(c)(3) than for a business. We will tell you plainly what a proper site costs and work with you on scope, timeline, and payment structure to fit a board-approved budget, rather than quoting our standard commercial rate and hoping.
