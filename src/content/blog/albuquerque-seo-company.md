## What SEO actually is

Search engine optimization is the work of making a website the best available answer to what someone typed into Google, and making sure Google can tell.

That splits into three parts:

**Technical.** Can Google crawl your site, read it, and load it quickly? Speed, mobile rendering, site structure, and structured data all sit here. This is the least glamorous part and often the part with the biggest immediate payoff, because it is where sites are usually broken.

**Content.** Do you have pages that genuinely answer what people search for? Not pages that mention the keyword, but pages that answer the question. A page about "restaurant website design" that explains online ordering, menu updates, and what it costs will outperform a page that says "we are the best restaurant website designers" fifty times over.

**Authority.** Do other credible sites reference you? This is the slowest part and the one most vulnerable to bad practice, because buying links is easy and getting them honestly is not.

## The thing most Albuquerque businesses do not know

For a search like "SEO company in Albuquerque" or "plumber near me", Google shows a **map pack**, three local businesses with a map, above the normal blue-link results.

That block frequently gets more clicks than the first organic result. And it is not driven by your website.

The map pack is driven by your **Google Business Profile**, your **reviews**, and your **proximity to the person searching**. You could have the best-optimized website in New Mexico and still be invisible in the map pack if your profile is half-finished and you have four reviews.

This matters enormously for a local business, and it is the single most common gap we find. If an SEO company talks to you about your website for an hour without asking about your Google Business Profile or your review count, they are not thinking about local search. They are thinking about search in general, which is a different job.

**What to do about it, whether or not you hire anyone:**

- Claim your Google Business Profile and complete every field, including services, hours, and photos
- Get reviews consistently, not in one burst. Ask every satisfied customer
- Reply to reviews, including the unhappy ones
- Make sure your name, address, and phone number are identical everywhere they appear online
- Post updates occasionally, because it is a signal that the business is active

None of that costs money. All of it outranks a lot of paid work.

## What good SEO work looks like

**It starts with an audit, not a proposal.** Anyone who quotes a price before looking at your site is guessing. The first conversation should involve them asking questions about your business, not describing packages.

**It fixes the foundation first.** Speed, mobile rendering, crawl errors, and broken pages come before anything else. There is no point writing new content for a site Google struggles to read.

**It targets what people actually search for.** There is often a wide gap between how a business describes itself and the words its customers use. Finding that gap is most of keyword research.

**It measures honestly.** Rankings, traffic, and the one that matters, enquiries. A report full of impressions and "engagement" while the phone stays quiet is a report designed to look busy.

**It compounds.** Good SEO work keeps paying after you stop, because the pages and the authority remain. That is the fundamental difference from advertising, which stops the day you stop paying.

## Red flags

**"We guarantee first-page rankings."** Nobody controls Google's results. This is the clearest single signal to walk away.

**Long contracts with early lock-in.** Twelve months signed before you have seen anything is a structure designed to protect the agency, not to serve you. Reasonable companies earn renewal.

**No explanation of the work.** If they cannot describe what they will do in plain language, either they do not know or they are hiding something. Usually it is link buying.

**Reports without enquiries.** Traffic that never becomes a phone call is a vanity metric. Ask directly: how many enquiries came from this last month?

**Cheap monthly packages with no audit.** SEO priced like a subscription, sold before anyone has looked at your site, is almost always automated directory submissions and spun content. It can actively harm you.

**Nobody local has heard of them.** Albuquerque is not a big market. Ask around.

## Questions worth asking

Take these to any company you are considering, including us:

1. **What would you do first, and why?** A good answer is specific to your site. A bad one is a description of their process.
2. **Who will actually do the work?** Some agencies sell locally and outsource offshore. That is not automatically bad, but you should know.
3. **How do you build links?** If the answer is vague, assume they buy them. Bought links carry real risk of a penalty.
4. **What does success look like in six months?** It should be phrased in enquiries or revenue, not rankings.
5. **What have you done for a business like mine?** Ask for specifics you can verify.
6. **What happens if I leave?** Do you keep the content, the analytics, the Google Business Profile access? The answer should be yes to all three.

## AEO: getting found when customers ask AI

A growing share of searches never reach a results page at all. Someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or Google's own AI overview for "a good web designer in Albuquerque", gets three names, and calls one of them. There is no page two. There is barely a page one.

This is Answer Engine Optimization, and it is the most significant shift in how businesses get found since mobile search. Most Albuquerque agencies are not yet talking about it.

### How it differs from SEO

Traditional SEO competes for a **position**. AEO competes to be **cited**.

That difference matters more than it sounds. A search result gives you ten chances to be clicked; an AI answer typically names two or three businesses and nobody else exists. Being fourth-best in an AI answer is the same as being invisible.

It also changes what wins. Ranking has always rewarded authority and links. Citation rewards *clarity*, being the source that most unambiguously states a fact the assistant needs.

### What actually works

**Answer questions directly.** An assistant looking for "how much does a website cost in Albuquerque" cannot cite a page that says "contact us for a quote". It will cite whoever gave a real answer. This is the single biggest opportunity for most local businesses, because almost everyone hides behind a contact form.

**Structured data.** Schema markup tells a machine unambiguously what your page is: a business, its address, its services, its opening hours, its reviews, a specific question with a specific answer. Humans infer this from layout; machines need it stated.

**Consistency everywhere.** Assistants cross-check. If your address differs between your website, your Google Business Profile, and an old directory listing, you become a less reliable source than a competitor whose details agree everywhere.

**Facts an assistant can quote.** "Founded in 1995." "Based at 500 Marquette Ave NW." "Certified Webflow Partner." Concrete, checkable, and specific. Marketing adjectives such as leading, premier, and innovative are unquotable and are ignored.

**A crawlable, fast site.** AI crawlers are less patient than Google's. A site that renders its content only after JavaScript runs may simply not be read.

### How to check whether it is working

Ask the assistants yourself. Open ChatGPT or Gemini and ask what you would want a customer to ask, such as "who does web design in Albuquerque" or "best restaurant website designer in New Mexico", and see whether you are named, whether the details are right, and who is named instead.

It is a crude test, and results vary between people and sessions. It is also the only feedback loop currently available, and it is more informative than most rank-tracking reports.

Worth knowing: we can see this happening in our own Search Console data. Queries that read like assistant lookups, long and oddly phrased and structured like a machine's question rather than a person's, now appear against our pages and rank first. AI systems are already asking Google about Albuquerque businesses.

### The overlap

The reassuring part: perhaps eighty per cent of AEO is good SEO done properly. Clear structure, fast pages, genuine answers, accurate structured data, consistent business information. Do those and you are most of the way there.

The remaining part is a change of mindset: writing to be *quoted* rather than to be *ranked*. It means shorter, more direct answers near the top of a page, factual specifics instead of adjectives, and a willingness to state prices and processes plainly that a lot of businesses find uncomfortable.

## Should you just do it yourself?

For a lot of Albuquerque small businesses, honestly, partly yes.

The Google Business Profile work, the reviews, and the consistent business information are free, high-impact, and do not need an agency. If you do nothing else, do those.

Where hiring makes sense is the technical repair, the content that takes hours you do not have, and having someone watch the numbers and tell you what changed. Those are the parts that need either expertise or time you would rather spend running your business.
