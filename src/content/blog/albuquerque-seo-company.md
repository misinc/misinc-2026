Those phrases do not tell you what anyone will actually do.

## What SEO actually involves

Search engine optimization is the work of making useful pages easier for search engines to find, understand, and present for relevant searches.

Most of the work falls into three connected areas:

**Technical foundations.** Can search engines crawl and index the pages that matter? Do redirects, canonical URLs, mobile behavior, performance, and structured data work as intended?

**Content.** Does the site answer the questions customers are actually asking? Clear service pages, useful supporting material, sensible headings, and internal links matter more than repeating a keyword.

**Reputation and prominence.** Is the business referenced by credible websites, directories, partners, customers, and local organizations? This develops more slowly and is also where shortcuts create the most risk.

A proposal that covers only one of these areas may be appropriate for a narrow problem. It should not be presented as a complete SEO program unless it genuinely is one.

## Local search is a different part of the job

For searches with local intent, Google may show a map and a group of nearby businesses above or among the ordinary results.

Google describes the main local ranking factors as **relevance, distance, and prominence**:

- **Relevance** is how well the business matches the search.
- **Distance** is how far the business is from the person searching, or from the location named in the search.
- **Prominence** reflects how well known and well referenced the business is.

Your Google Business Profile is important, but it does not operate in isolation. Reviews, the accuracy of your public information, the content of your website, and references from other websites can all contribute.

Practical work you can do without hiring anyone:

- Claim and verify your Google Business Profile.
- Complete the services, hours, contact information, categories, and photographs.
- Ask satisfied customers for honest reviews over time.
- Reply to reviews professionally.
- Keep your name, address, phone number, hours, and website consistent across important listings.

If an SEO company discusses only your website and never asks about your Business Profile, reviews, service area, or local competition, it may be overlooking a large part of the opportunity.

## What good SEO work looks like

**It begins with diagnosis.** Published starting prices are useful, but a meaningful scope requires someone to inspect the site, understand the business, and identify the actual problem.

**It protects what already works.** Existing pages, links, and URLs may carry years of search history. A redesign or content cleanup should start by identifying what already earns visibility.

**It explains priorities.** A useful audit separates urgent technical problems from worthwhile improvements and low-value busywork.

**It targets intent, not just phrases.** The language a business uses internally may differ from the language customers use. Research should connect those two without turning the copy into a list of keywords.

**It measures what is available.** Rankings and search traffic provide context. Calls, forms, purchases, bookings, and qualified inquiries are closer to the business result, when the necessary tracking is in place.

**It reports uncertainty honestly.** Search results vary, attribution is imperfect, and not every change produces an isolated measurable outcome. A responsible report says what changed, what moved, and what remains uncertain.

## Warning signs

**Guaranteed rankings.** Nobody outside Google controls Google’s results.

**A long commitment before any work is demonstrated.** Some programs genuinely need time, but the company should still be accountable for clear work and reporting along the way.

**Vague descriptions of link building.** Ask where links will come from and why those sources are appropriate. Manufactured or paid link schemes can create risk rather than durable authority.

**Reports designed around favorable-looking numbers.** Impressions can rise without generating a single useful visit. The report should connect visibility to the goals of the business wherever possible.

**Content produced at scale without expertise.** Publishing many thin pages is not a substitute for answering a smaller number of real questions well.

**No access to your own accounts.** Your Business Profile, Search Console, analytics, content, and other client accounts should remain under your control.

## Questions worth asking

1. **What would you investigate first, and why?** The answer should relate to your site and market.
2. **Who will do the work?** You should know whether implementation is handled by the person you meet, another employee, or an outside contractor.
3. **How will you earn links or mentions?** The answer should be specific enough to evaluate.
4. **What can we reasonably measure?** This exposes whether the reporting plan fits your actual website and sales process.
5. **What have you done for a comparable business?** Relevant experience matters more than an impressive but unrelated client list.
6. **What happens if we stop working together?** You should retain your content, data, and account access.
7. **Which results are outside your control?** A good company should be comfortable answering this plainly.

## What SEO costs

Some SEO work is project-shaped. A technical audit, migration review, or defined set of repairs can have a beginning and an end.

Other work is ongoing because competitors publish, websites change, listings become inaccurate, and new questions emerge. That is why retainers are common.

## Where AEO fits

People increasingly encounter businesses through AI-assisted search experiences as well as traditional results. The practical work is to make accurate information accessible, consistent, and useful across those environments.

That overlaps with SEO, but it is not a guaranteed path to a citation. Search and AI systems decide which sources to use, and those decisions can vary between products, sessions, and questions.

Our separate [Answer Engine Optimization service](/services/aeo) focuses on crawler access, clear business facts, appropriate structured data, consistency across public sources, and monitoring representative questions. We do not sell guaranteed citations or claim that a particular markup forces an assistant to recommend a business.

If a company talks about secret AEO formulas, guaranteed mentions, or special schema that makes AI systems choose you, ask for evidence.

## Should you do some of it yourself?

For many Albuquerque businesses, yes.

Maintain the Business Profile. Ask for reviews. Correct inconsistent listings. Write down the questions customers ask before they buy and answer them on the appropriate pages.

Hiring becomes useful when the site needs technical diagnosis, when important pages are missing, when nobody has time to implement the work, or when you need someone to watch the evidence and adjust the priorities.
